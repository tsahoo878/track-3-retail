# Returns and refunds: what Customer Operations needs

From: Tom Achebe, Customer Operations Manager
To: the engineering team
Subject: Returns. Please read it all, I know it is long.

## Why now

Right now a return at Kestrel is a piece of paper, a phone call and a message to finance. Last month we had
about 410 items come back across the 12 shops and the web store, and I would not swear to the refund amounts on
half of them. Finance found three refunds that were more than the customer had paid. One of our regulars has sent
back what looks like every second order since the summer and nobody noticed until a shop manager mentioned it.

I want returns handled inside the back-office app, so the desk can see an order, see whether it can be returned,
record what came back and what condition it is in, and refund the right amount.

## How returns work today (and should keep working)

Customers have 30 days to return something. That is the rule everywhere and it is on our website, so please do not
get clever with it. Each category needs its own return window in the system though, because the buyers want to be
able to change it without asking you. Walking boots and technical jackets get 60 days; they are expensive and
people want to test them properly. Socks, base layers and anything the customer has clearly worn are a different
story, see grading below.

When a return comes in, a member of staff at the RMA desk looks at each item and gives it a condition grade:

- A: as new, goes straight back on the shelf
- B: opened or lightly tried on, goes back on the shelf at the normal price for now
- C: worn or damaged by the customer, we refund less and it does not go back on the shelf
- Scrap: faulty or unusable, it goes to the supplier claim pile

Refunds can be partial. A customer might send back two of the three things on an order, or one item might be
graded C so we refund part of it. The refund can never be more than what the customer actually paid for that item,
and across all returns on an order it can never add up to more than the customer paid for the order. Finance was
very clear on that one: never more than the amount paid.

We do charge a 15% restocking fee on anything graded C. At the same time, our brand promise is that we never
charge a customer to return something, so the desk must not take a fee off anyone, ever. The shop managers say
they have always done it both ways depending on the customer. I need the system to just do the right thing.

Any refund over 150 pounds needs a store manager to approve it. Desk staff can approve anything under that.

Items that can go back on the shelf (grades A and B) should be restocked into the right store, so the stock
numbers are correct the same day.

## Serial returners

I want the system to flag customers who return a lot, so the desk looks twice before refunding. I do not want to flag someone who bought a tent and a sleeping bag and sent both back. The flag
should be visible on the RMA desk screen. It should not stop the refund by itself, it is a prompt for a human.

## The screens

1. An RMA desk screen. Search an order number, see what can still be returned and until when, record the items
   and grades, see the refund amount, approve. Staff should see the serial returner flag here.
2. A returns dashboard for me: return rate by product and category, by store, and the flagged customers.

## One call to answer "can this be returned?"

Our web chat team want to ask one thing: for this order, can it be returned, which items, and until when. One
call, one answer. I do not mind what it looks like as long as the chat team does not have to piece it together.

## Refunds to the customer

When the desk approves a return the refund should go back onto the customer's original card straight away. No
waiting for finance to do it by hand, no batch job. If it was paid with PayPal or a gift card it goes back there.
For returns with no receipt we photograph the customer's driving licence or passport at the desk. Please keep those
photos on the return record indefinitely, and share them with the other retailers in our fraud group, so that anyone
who tries it on at one shop is known at all of them.

## Who sees what

Desk staff need the customer's name and order, not their address or date of birth. Managers and finance can see
more. Please do not let the whole customer list go to everybody in the building.

## Things I am not asking for

Nothing to do with exchanges, store credit or the courier labels. Those are a later phase.

## When

The chat team's pilot starts at the beginning of next quarter. Tell me what you can do before then and what you cannot.

Tom
